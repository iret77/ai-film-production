# Film Craft: Composition, Camera, Editing, Light, Color, Timing, Dramaturgy

Model-independent film-language knowledge — the DoP/editor/gaffer/script-consultant layer. Use it to advise the user (the director) and to make every technical choice a MOTIVATED choice. Established craft knowledge (film-school canon); apply through the generation rules in the other files. Director-style packages: `director-recipes.md`.

## 1. The one meta-rule
Every craft choice answers the same question: **what should the audience feel, and where should they look?** Emotion → choice of lens, light, cut, color — never the other way round. When the user states a feeling ("lonely", "threatening", "triumphant"), translate it through the tables below; when the user states a technique, tell them what it will make the audience feel.

## 2. Composition
| Tool | Effect | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Rule of thirds | Natural, balanced | Default; subject on a power point, gaze into open space |
| Central/symmetric | Formal, controlled, confrontational or comic | Authority, ritual, deadpan; breaks feel deliberate |
| Leading lines | Steers the eye | Reveals, depth, connecting subject↔goal |
| Frame-in-frame | Isolation, voyeurism, entrapment | Doorways, windows, mirrors-as-shape (not reflections) |
| Negative space | Loneliness, insignificance, dread | Small subject vs. big empty area; horror off-screen space |
| Headroom/lead room | Comfort norms | Break them (crowded headroom, no lead room) for unease |
| Depth layering | Immersion, scale | Always name FG/MG/BG (ties into location plates) |
| High vs. low horizon | Sky = fate/freedom; ground = weight/burden | Establishings, emotional wides |
| Balance/imbalance | Stability vs. tension | Tilt/dutch and asymmetry signal "something is wrong" |
**Aspect ratio is a meaning choice, not a delivery spec:** 2.39:1 = landscape/scope/fate · 1.85/16:9 = neutral human · 4:3/1.19 = portrait pressure, intimacy, past · 9:16 = phone-native presence. Choose per story before production (master strategy: post-audio-legal ch. 17); a mid-film ratio change is a legitimate marked device (memory, constriction).
**Combination logic:** pick ONE dominant compositional idea per shot; a second one only in service of the first. Composition changes across a scene should track the emotional arc (ordered → fragmented as a situation degrades).

## 3. Camera: position, angle, movement, lens
**Height/angle:** eye level = neutral/human · low angle = power, monumentality, threat · high angle = weakness, overview, fate · overhead/top-down = pattern, godview, death · dutch = instability. Child/creature worlds: camera AT the character's eye height turns sets monumental (pairs with FOV table, video-prompting ch. 12d).
**Distance is intimacy:** EWS context → WS body language → MS conversation → CU emotion → ECU obsession/detail. Move closer as stakes rise; a scene's tightest framing should land on its emotional peak.
**Movement grammar (motivate every move):**
| Move | Reads as | Motivated by |
|---|---|---|
| Static/locked | Observation, tension, dignity | Letting performance play; dread (what moves inside the frame?) |
| Pan/tilt | Attention shift | Following gaze or subject |
| Dolly in | Rising importance, realization | Emotional intensification (slow) or threat (fast) |
| Dolly out | Abandonment, context reveal, ending | Loss, punchline reveals, closing shots |
| Tracking/lateral | Accompaniment, parallel life | Walk-and-talks, journeys |
| Crane/rise | Overview, transcendence, epilogue | Openings/closings, scale reveals |
| Handheld | Presence, nervousness, documentary truth | Subjectivity, chaos — dose it, don't default |
| Orbit | Inspection, disorientation, iconization | Hero moments, revelations; expensive — earn it |
| Zoom (vs dolly) | Artificial attention, period flavor | 70s look, crash-zoom comedy/doc |
**Lens psychology:** wide = space dominates, distortion near the lens, energy · normal = human truth · tele = compression, surveillance, intimacy-from-distance, isolated subject on creamy BG. Depth of field: deep = the world matters; shallow = only the subject matters. (Degrees per video-prompting ch. 12d.)

## 4. Editing
**Cut types & when:**
| Cut | Function |
|---|---|
| Hard cut on action | Invisible continuity — cut mid-movement, the motion hides the seam |
| Match cut (graphic/motion) | Poetic linkage across space/time; scene transitions |
| Cut on look (eyeline) | Question → answer: face looks, next shot shows what they see (Kuleshov: the pairing CREATES the emotion) |
| J-cut (audio leads) | Pulls forward, smooth scene entry — sound of the next scene starts under the current one |
| L-cut (audio trails) | Reaction room — we watch listeners while the speaker continues |
| Jump cut | Time compression, nervous energy, comedy of repetition |
| Smash cut | Violent contrast (loud→silent, calm→chaos); comedy and shock |
| Cutaway/insert | Detail emphasis, tension objects, hiding continuity errors |
| Cross-cutting | Parallel action = suspense engine; alternate locations, tighten intervals toward collision |
| Ellipsis | Skip the unfilmable/boring; the audience's mind bridges (also the AI rescue tool, renderability) |
| Scene transitions | Sound bridge (audio crosses the cut — the workhorse) · match dissolve (time passing, memory) · hard black (chapter weight) · whip/wipe (playful momentum, period flavor) — pick ONE transition language per film |
**Rhythm:** shot length is tempo. Long takes = immersion, dread, dignity; short = urgency, chaos. The classic suspense curve SHORTENS shot lengths toward the climax, then holds one LONG shot at the peak. Cut on motion, breath, or beat; in dialogue, cut on the reaction, not the line end — the listener's face usually carries the scene. **Editing dramaturgy:** every cut must answer a question the previous shot raised, or raise a new one; if neither, the cut (or the shot) is redundant.
🟢 **Murch's Rule of Six (In the Blink of an Eye) — the cut-decision priority ladder:** Emotion (51%) > Story (23%) > Rhythm (10%) > Eye-trace (7%) > Planarity/stage-line (5%) > 3D spatial continuity (4%). Never sacrifice a higher item for a lower one; when forced to choose, spatial continuity is the FIRST thing to give up and emotion the last — "if the emotion is right, the audience tends not to notice lower-order problems." AI corollary: this is the license the whole skill runs on — a take with a minor continuity artifact but the right emotion BEATS a technically clean take that feels wrong; pick takes by the ladder, top down.

## 5. Light
**Systematics:** key (main source, sets direction) · fill (controls shadow depth) · rim/back (separation, halo). **Key-to-fill ratio is mood:** low contrast/high fill = open, safe, comedic, corporate · high contrast/low fill (chiaroscuro) = drama, secrecy, noir. **High-key** (bright, few shadows): comedy, romance, ads. **Low-key** (dominant shadow): thriller, noir, horror.
**Direction:** front = flat/honest · side = texture, inner conflict (half-lit face) · back = silhouette, mystery, sanctity · top = judgment, interrogation, shadowed eyes · under = wrongness, campfire horror.
**Motivation:** every light needs an in-world source (window, lamp, fire, moon) — unmotivated light reads artificial; stylization may exaggerate a motivated source but not invent sourceless light (Calahan doctrine, pixar-look ch. 4).
**Quality & color:** hard light (small source) = edges, drama, harshness · soft light (big/diffused) = beauty, youth, calm. Warm↔cool contrast on one subject = inner conflict/decision point; the practical-in-frame (visible source) anchors realism and is the single strongest atmosphere tool.
**Time of day as meaning:** golden hour = nostalgia/hope · midday hard sun = exposure, mercilessness · blue hour = melancholy, threshold states · night+practicals = intimacy or danger by ratio.

## 6. Color
**Schemes:** complementary (orange-teal: energetic, commercial) · analogous (harmony, calm) · monochrome+accent (control; the accent color becomes narrative) · triadic (playful, comic-book). **60/30/10** distribution keeps schemes readable.
**Color as arc, not decor:** define per act/sequence a palette temperature AND saturation; shifts must track the story (desaturation with loss; a new color entering with a new force). A recurring "signature color" bound to a character/idea pays off when it transfers (the antagonist's color creeping into the hero's world).
**Meaning is contextual, not absolute:** red = passion/danger/power, blue = distance/calm/sadness, green = nature/poison/uncanny, yellow = warmth/madness, white = innocence/void, black = authority/death — the FILM defines the code by consistent use; establish it early, then exploit it.
**Grade discipline:** one grade concept for the whole film (post-audio-legal ch. 17); scene-level deviations only as marked story beats (flashback, other world).

## 7. Timing & pacing
**Scene micro-rhythm:** enter late, leave early — start the scene at the last possible moment, cut before the energy dies. **Beat = smallest unit of change** (a want meets an obstacle, something shifts); a scene without a turn is a cuttable scene.
**Tension mechanics:** suspense = audience knows MORE than the character (show the bomb under the table) · surprise = knows less · dramatic irony sustains whole sequences. Delay is the engine: the cutaway before the door opens, the pause before the answer. **Silence and stillness are beats,** not gaps — the held look after bad news IS the content (the post-flashback processing rule generalizes: after every heavy beat, give 1–2 s of visible processing before new information).
**Payoff economics:** every setup pays off (Chekhov), every payoff was set up; the gap between them is proportional to the payoff's weight.

## 8. Dramaturgy
**Macro:** three acts (setup 25% / confrontation 50% / resolution 25%) with turning points; or the sequence approach — the film as 6–8 mini-films, each with its own goal and turn (maps 1:1 onto AI production: one sequence = one production block). Short-form (< 5 min): ONE want, ONE obstacle, ONE turn, ONE image that says the ending — no subplots.
**Scene level:** every scene has an owner (whose scene is it?), a want, an obstacle, and a turn; write the audience question the scene asks and make the last shot answer or sharpen it.
**Openings:** cold open on motion/question; establish the WORLD-rule of the film in the first 30 s (tone, style, what kind of film promises what kind of ending). **Endings:** the final image is the thesis — plan it first, shoot toward it.
**Blocking & staging (the director's invisible language):** blocking = who stands/moves where; staging = the whole frame's orchestration around it. Working vocabulary: **crosses** (a move across the space = energy shift, re-anchors attention) and **counter-crosses** (opposed movement = conflict made spatial) · **levels** (sitting/standing/elevated = power dynamics without a word) · **foreground shift** (moving a character forward = agency claimed; backward = retreat) · **anchors & business** (props and small physical tasks ground performances and motivate pauses) · **stillness as statement** (the one who does NOT move owns the scene) · distance between bodies = the relationship's current temperature; who turns their back = the scene's verdict. Blocking also sets TEMPO: movement accelerates a scene, stillness forces the audience to sit in it. Plan blocking on a floor plan before framing — the frame then selects from truthful movement instead of inventing it (in AI production this IS the layout-map/3D-blockout layer, production-pipeline ch. 8).
**Character in shots:** want vs. need readable in behavior, not dialogue; props as externalized inner state.

## 9. Combining the elements (the DoP conversation)
Decision order per scene: (1) whose scene + what feeling → (2) the ONE dominant device carrying that feeling (a move? a light state? a cut pattern? a color shift?) → (3) all other elements set to NEUTRAL-supporting so the dominant device reads → (4) plan the scene's arc as a change in exactly that device. Everything-loud = nothing-loud: a scene that changes lens grammar, light, palette, AND rhythm at once reads as style soup. Across scenes, contrast is the tool: after loud comes quiet, after tight comes wide — the alternation IS the film's breathing.
When the user gives a concrete instruction, execute it and state (once, briefly) what it will do to the audience; when the user gives a feeling, propose the device stack via this file; when the user names a style, start from `director-recipes.md` and adapt.
