# Director Recipes: Combined Style Packages (Appendix)

Optional starting points, NOT a replacement for film-craft.md — each recipe combines composition, camera, editing, light, color, and timing into one coherent package a project can adopt and adapt. Legally these are aesthetic lineages ("in the spirit of, fully original execution" — the established formula, platforms-models ch. 13); never reproduce protected characters, titles-as-content, or scenes. Offer recipes when the user wants a fast style decision; mix at most TWO, with one dominant.

Format per recipe: Feels like → Composition · Camera · Editing · Light · Color · Timing · Sound cue · Best for.

**Wes Anderson (symmetry & deadpan)** — Feels like a storybook diorama with dry wit. Composition: dead-center symmetry, planimetric (flat-on) tableaus, meticulous production design. Camera: locked-off or precise 90° whip pans, lateral tracking like page-turns. Editing: rhythmic, chapter cards, inserts of objects/hands. Light: soft, even, almost shadowless. Color: pastel palettes, one accent per world. Timing: metronomic; deadpan holds after punchlines. Sound: needle-drops, narrated formality. Best for: whimsy, families, melancholy comedy, stylized worlds.

**David Fincher (surgical control)** — Feels like being watched by something patient. Composition: geometric, ruled lines, subject boxed by architecture. Camera: the "silent machine" — motivated micro-moves, no handheld tremor, camera height at subject logic. Editing: invisible, information-dense, cuts on cognition. Light: low-key, top-ish soft sources, shadowed eyes, greenish-yellow practicals. Color: desaturated teal/amber/sickly green. Timing: relentless even pulse, no wasted frame. Sound: low ambience beds. Best for: thrillers, procedurals, tech dread.

**Roger Deakins lineage (DoP grammar, Mendes class)** — Feels like monumental clarity. Composition: vast negative space, tiny humans in geometry, silhouettes against gradients. Camera: slow disciplined moves, long holds, one idea per shot. Editing: patient, cuts as chapter beats. Light: single motivated source pushed to its limit (fire, window slab, sodium). Color: constrained near-monochrome fields (dust orange, fog gray, night blue). Timing: dread through duration. Sound: bass swells, silence weaponized. Best for: epic sci-fi, war, existential scale.

**Stanley Kubrick (one-point dread)** — Feels like fate has a floor plan. Composition: one-point perspective corridors, ruthless symmetry. Camera: slow dolly/steadicam glides, wide lenses close to faces. Editing: long takes, then brutal cuts; classical music against horror. Light: motivated-but-exaggerated (candles, fluorescents), often flat-wide. Color: era-saturated, single-color rooms as psychology. Timing: glacial escalation. Best for: horror, hubris, institutions.

**Steven Spielberg (invisible blockbuster grammar)** — Feels like being told a story by a master who never shows his hands. Composition: depth-staged frames (action on three planes at once), faces framed against wonder or threat. Camera: the "oner" — long takes where BLOCKING creates new framings instead of cuts (actors and camera reposition into fresh compositions); slow push-in on a realizing face (the "Spielberg face"); low angles at child eye-height for awe. Editing: invisible continuity, reaction before reveal — we see the face FIRST, then what it sees (builds anticipation); geography always clear. Light: motivated god-rays, backlit door/silhouette entrances, warm practicals against cool night. Color: naturalistic warmth, amber nostalgia vs. steel threat. Timing: suspense by withholding the full view of the threat (show pieces, delay the whole); sentiment earned through delay, then held. Sound: score carries emotion openly, silence before impact. Best for: adventure, family wonder, awe-and-threat storytelling, anything that must feel effortlessly classical.

**Denis Villeneuve (slow monumental dread)** — Feels like standing before something vast that does not care about you. Composition: extreme scale contrast (tiny human vs. megastructure/landscape), silhouettes against gradient voids, brutalist geometry, fog/dust as depth. Camera: glacial dolly and crane moves with absolute discipline, long approach shots that let dread accumulate, aerials as chapter punctuation. Editing: very long takes, cuts as slow heartbeats; information released in measured drops. Light: single massive motivated sources (a slab of window light, a dust-orange sky), deep underexposure trusted to hold. Color: near-monochrome atmospheric fields per sequence (ochre, slate, pale gray), desaturated humanity inside them. Timing: tension through DURATION and approach, not cutting; the pause before contact is the scene. Sound: sub-bass drones and BRAAM-class hits fused with score, silence as pressure. Best for: sci-fi of scale, first contact, institutional dread, existential thrillers. (Shares the Deakins DoP grammar above; Villeneuve adds the slower macro-rhythm, scale obsession, and sound-design fusion.)

**Danny Boyle (kinetic euphoria)** — Feels like adrenaline with a heartbeat. Composition: canted and wide-angle-close frames, subjects pinned to frame edges, bold graphic crops. Camera: restless energy — snap zooms, whip pans, low wide lenses running WITH characters; digital/available-light textures embraced. Editing: fast rhythmic montage cut to music, freeze-frames with inserted titles/graphics, time-lapse and speed shifts, fantasy inserts crashing into reality. Light: hard saturated practicals (sodium, neon, fluorescent), unashamed mixed color temperatures. Color: hyper-saturated pop palettes — acid greens, oranges, cyans. Timing: sprint-and-drop — euphoric acceleration, then a sudden held beat of stillness or horror; needle-drop as structural device. Sound: driving contemporary tracks fused to cut rhythm. Best for: urban energy, addiction/obsession stories, survival against the clock, montage-driven arcs.

**Paul Greengrass (chaos verité)** — Feels like being inside the event. Composition: none held for long — found framings, crops, obstruction. Camera: handheld urgency, whip refocus, snap zooms. Editing: fast fragmentary, geography sacrificed for pulse (keep ONE orienting master per beat or the audience truly loses the room). Light: available/practical, unpolished. Color: newsprint desaturation. Timing: accelerando to impact. Best for: action, thrillers, docu-drama. (AI note: this style ABSORBS model artifacts — a legitimate rescue look.)

**Sergio Leone (opera of faces)** — Feels like myth stretched over minutes. Composition: extreme wides ↔ extreme close-ups (eyes), horizon staging. Camera: slow push-ins to duel framings, crane reveals. Editing: elastic time — long waiting, explosive resolution in seconds. Light: hard sun, dust, deep hat shadows. Color: bleached earth tones. Timing: the wait IS the scene; score and cut in lockstep. Best for: westerns, standoffs, revenge arcs.

**Wong Kar-wai (romantic blur)** — Feels like memory in a rainy neon city. Composition: frames within frames, mirrors-as-shape, bodies cropped. Camera: handheld intimacy, step-printed smear on motion. Editing: elliptical, repetition as longing, voiceover over time-jumps. Light: neon practicals, smoke, wet reflections. Color: saturated reds/greens/golds. Timing: languid loops, sudden accelerations. Sound: repeated musical motif as memory. Best for: romance, longing, city nocturnes.

**Christopher Nolan (engineered time)** — Feels like a mechanism assembling. Composition: IMAX-scale clean wides, functional close-ups. Camera: mounted realism, aerial majesty, minimal ornament. Editing: cross-cut timelines converging (the structure IS the suspense), hard sound-bridge transitions. Light: naturalistic, cool. Color: steel blue/gray with warm human pockets. Timing: interleaved accelerando across parallel lines. Best for: heists, time mechanics, large-canvas thrillers.

**Hayao Miyazaki / Ghibli (animated humanism)** — Feels like wind you can trust. Composition: layered nature depth, characters small in living landscapes. Camera: calm pans, few cuts inside beats. Editing: "ma" — deliberate empty beats (riding, resting, weather) between plot beats; they ARE the film's breathing. Light: soft daylight, golden interiors. Color: watercolor greens/blues/creams (style-control §6 vocabulary). Timing: unhurried; action bursts read harder against calm. Best for: family, nature, quiet wonder. 

**Pixar house style** — covered as its own sourced look bible: pixar-look.md (believability-not-realism, simplexity, saturation dramaturgy, Calahan light, curated lens imperfection).

**Quentin Tarantino (pulp tension)** — Feels like a conversation that might kill you. Composition: low "trunk shot" angles, table geographies, foot-level inserts. Camera: slow creeping push-ins during dialogue, sudden crash zooms. Editing: chapters, long dialogue takes, violence in abrupt spurts; needle-drop cuts. Light: warm practicals, 70s glow. Color: saturated pulp warmth. Timing: extreme delay before release (the Leone debt). Best for: crime, dialogue-driven tension, genre homage.

**Alfred Hitchcock (pure suspense grammar)** — Feels like knowing too much. Composition: POV triads (look → seen → reaction), significant objects enlarged. Camera: dolly-in on realization, the vertigo shot for inner vertigo, god's-eye at moral distance. Editing: cross-cut bomb-under-the-table, cutaways that withhold. Light: hard noir keys on faces. Color: restrained with symbolic accents. Timing: audience ahead of character, always. Best for: thrillers, paranoia, one-location tension.

**Wim Wenders (the seeing road)** — Feels like travelling with someone who notices everything and hurries nothing. Composition: landscape as co-protagonist — figures small against horizons, signage, gas stations; frames that hold after people leave them. Camera: patient observational pans and drives (through-the-windshield as worldview), lateral tracking past facades; the Robby-Müller debt: available light trusted completely. Editing: unhurried, cuts follow attention not plot; scenes allowed to breathe past their "point". Light: real neon, dusk, motel practicals; color OR luminous B&W as a project-level decision. Color: Americana saturation (desert ochre, neon red, sky cyan) or silver-gray tenderness. Timing: journey rhythm — arrival, looking, moving on; dialogue sparse, listening long. Sound: diegetic radio/jukebox, slide-guitar loneliness. Best for: road movies, urban wandering, quiet redemption stories, documentary-fiction hybrids.

**Andrei Tarkovsky (sculpting in time)** — Feels like a dream remembering you. Composition: painterly depth, ruins and nature interpenetrating, figures dwarfed by rooms and weather. Camera: very slow drifting takes that reframe meaning within one shot; imperceptible push-ins over minutes. Editing: minimal — the take IS the edit; time pressure inside the shot, not between shots. Light: natural gloom, windows as altars, candle/fire as fragile life. Color: desaturated earth and sepia, shifts to B&W or color as memory states. Elements as recurring matter: water dripping and pooling, wind through grass, fire, milk, mirrors-as-idea. Timing: duration as spiritual instrument — hold until discomfort becomes contemplation. Best for: memory, faith, grief, poetic sci-fi. (AI note: demands long coherent takes — plan extension chains and accept a high reroll budget.)

**Yasujirō Ozu (domestic stillness)** — Feels like sitting with a family as seasons change. Composition: tatami-height camera (~90 cm), frontal symmetrical interiors, corridors and doorframes as frames, "pillow shots" — cutaways to objects/townscapes as breathing pauses. Camera: locked off, almost never moves; characters address nearly into the lens. Editing: 360°-space cutting (deliberately ignores the 180° rule — matched frontal singles instead), rhythmic regularity. Light: even, shadowless domestic daylight. Color: restrained interiors with one recurring red accent. Timing: ellipsis of the dramatic (deaths, weddings happen off-screen), attention on before and after. Best for: family drama, generational change, quiet heartbreak. (AI note: the frontal-singles system conveniently sidesteps reverse-angle continuity problems.)

**Ingmar Bergman (the face as landscape)** — Feels like being unable to look away from a soul. Composition: faces huge and centered, two faces overlapping in one frame (profile + frontal), stark figure-against-void. Camera: slow creep toward faces, long static holds on monologue. Editing: sparse; scenes as confessions played whole. Light: Nordic window softness against blackness, hard summer-island sun for exteriors; the face always modelled. Color: austere — chalk, black, blood red as eruption; or graphic B&W. Timing: silence between lines heavier than lines; dream inserts without warning. Best for: chamber drama, crisis of faith, marriages, illness. (AI note: lives on close-up performance — spend the acting/PERFORMANCE budget here.)

**Terrence Malick (whispered creation)** — Feels like memory praying. Composition: low sun through grass and hands, figures wading through nature, horizon-worship. Camera: wandering steadicam that circles and brushes past people, wide lenses close to skin, always moving gently. Editing: elliptical mosaic — moments not scenes, cut on emotional association; whispered voiceover stitches time. Light: magic hour as doctrine, backlit everything, no artificial feel. Color: golden wheat, deep green, sky pastel. Timing: drift; narrative dissolved into remembered fragments. Best for: memory pieces, nature-spiritual arcs, love and loss montage. (AI note: montage-of-moments structure fits generation limits unusually well — no long continuity chains.)

**David Lynch (dream logic)** — Feels like the familiar turned inside out. Composition: ordinary Americana framed a beat too long and too centered; curtains, corridors, stages. Camera: slow floating dollies into darkness, sudden static wrongness. Editing: scene logic obeys dream association, not causality; inserts of objects charged with menace. Light: pools of light in black voids, flickering practicals, red lamps. Color: saturated red/blue against wood-panel browns. Timing: scenes held past comfort; sound arrives before its source. Sound: room-tone drones, industrial hum, sudden silence — the true horror instrument. Best for: surreal thriller, uncanny small towns, subconscious narratives. (AI note: dream logic legitimizes discontinuity — model inconsistencies can read as intention; still lock the recurring faces.)

**UGC/found-footage (authorless camera)** — Feels like it really happened. The anti-recipe: composition accidental, camera device-logic (phone/bodycam/CCTV — one device per sequence, renderability §6b four-tell rule), editing in-camera cuts or none, light available-only, color device-processed, timing unshaped. Best for: horror, virality, testimony. (AI note: highest artifact tolerance of all styles.)


## DoP signatures (light/lens packages — combinable with any structure above)

DoP names are the established legal style currency ("References in spirit, fully original execution" — platforms-models ch. 13). Leaner format: pure image signatures without editing/dramaturgy; mix ONE DoP signature with ONE director structure. The Deakins package sits above as its own lineage recipe.

**Emmanuel Lubezki** — Natural light only, magic hour as religion; ultra-wide lenses inches from faces (distorted intimacy); flowing unbroken steadicam oners, camera as a body among bodies; sun flares hitting the lens as grace notes. Grade: naturalistic, luminous. Pairs with: Malick drift, survival epics, spiritual realism.
**Hoyte van Hoytema** — Monumental cool naturalism; IMAX-scale sharpness with soft-window softness on faces; restrained palette (slate, sand, sea gray); practical-driven interiors, giant single sources; aerial majesty without gloss. Pairs with: Nolan mechanisms, cold-war textures, sober sci-fi.
**Greig Fraser** — Texture-first low-light digital: skin grain, dust, breath visible; underexposure trusted deep into shadow; silhouettes against atmospheric voids; anamorphic softness with brutal contrast pockets. Grade: monochromatic warmth or bone-dry desaturation. Pairs with: Villeneuve scale, noir superhero, candlelit intrigue.
**Vittorio Storaro** — Color as ideology: each act OWNS a hue (red passion, blue reason, gold decadence); painterly artificial light unashamed of theatricality; symmetrical torch-and-lantern compositions; moving light DURING the shot as dramaturgy. Pairs with: opera-scale drama, psychedelic war, myth.
**Gordon Willis** — The prince of darkness: top-light that buries eyes in shadow (moral unreadability); amber-and-black interiors, whole frames underexposed but composed; static formal wides, power geometry. Pairs with: crime dynasties, institutional evil, 70s paranoia.
**Janusz Kamiński** — Blown backlit highlights through smoke/dust; desaturated silver skin (bleach-bypass feel); handheld combat realism OR godlight sentiment — both extremes, nothing between; hard shafts through windows. Pairs with: Spielberg grammar, war memory, testimony.
**Christopher Doyle** — Handheld color fever: saturated neon smear, step-printed motion trails; cramped interiors shot through glass, rain, fabric; available fluorescents mixed hot; frames cropped by architecture. Pairs with: Wong Kar-wai longing, urban fever dreams, romantic disorientation.
**Sven Nykvist** — The face light standard: soft window-side modelling, two faces one frame, nothing decorative; Nordic gray exteriors, candle-warm interiors; invisible technique in total service of performance. Pairs with: Bergman chamber work, intimate drama, illness and faith.
**Bradford Young** — Deep soft darkness with protected skin tones; underexposed frames that stay tender, not grim; practical pools in black rooms; haze halos around single sources. Grade: warm dark browns, muted jewel accents. Pairs with: quiet sci-fi, intimate histories, night interiors.


## Harmony map: which packages combine

Real-world partnerships are the proof that these pairings work — directors keep their DoPs because structure and image philosophy complete each other. Use the documented pairs as safe defaults, the cross-pairings as informed experiments, and respect the clash list.

**Documented partnerships (safe defaults):**
| Director structure | DoP signature | Why it harmonizes |
|---|---|---|
| Spielberg | Kamiński | Blown godlight + smoke gives the wonder-and-threat grammar its sentiment and its war-memory grit — both extremes in one toolkit |
| Nolan | Hoytema | Monumental sober naturalism scales to IMAX mechanisms without gloss; cool restraint matches engineered structure |
| Villeneuve | Deakins → Fraser | Deakins built the monumental-clarity phase; Fraser's texture-first underexposure carries the later dust-and-dread scale — both serve duration-dread |
| Wong Kar-wai | Doyle | Step-printed neon smear IS the longing; structure and image are inseparable here — adopt them together or not at all |
| Bergman | Nykvist | The face-as-landscape structure needs the face-light standard; nothing decorative survives the chamber |
| Malick | Lubezki | Wandering whisper-montage requires a camera that lives in natural light and moves like breath |
| Wenders | (Robby Müller lineage) | Available-light Americana trust — the seeing-road structure with neon-and-dusk patience |
| Kubrick / Tarantino / Fincher | (house signatures) | Their image grammar is baked into the director recipe itself — no external DoP package needed; adding one dilutes |

**Recommended cross-pairings (coherent experiments):**
- Hitchcock structure × Willis light — suspense grammar in moral darkness (paranoia thrillers).
- Leone timing × Storaro color — operatic waiting with ideological hues (myth, revenge).
- Boyle kinetics × Doyle fever — double adrenaline for urban delirium; keep ONE calm anchor scene or it exhausts.
- Ozu stillness × Nykvist faces — domestic chamber pieces of great tenderness.
- Lynch dream logic × Bradford Young darkness — soft uncanny instead of harsh horror.
- Tarkovsky duration × Fraser texture — modern low-light poetry (grief, faith, slow sci-fi).
- Greengrass verité × Kamiński combat realism — war/action documentary punch.
- Anderson symmetry × Storaro color — heightened storybook theatricality (dose it; two maximalists).

**Clashes (don't combine):**
- Lubezki natural-light flow × Fincher/Kubrick control — improvised light against surgical structure contradicts both.
- Storaro theatricality × Greengrass/UGC verité — ideological color dies in found-footage logic.
- Willis darkness × Miyazaki/Pixar family warmth — buried eyes against readable faces.
- Doyle smear × Nolan mechanism — motion fever blurs the machine's legibility.
- More than TWO packages total, ever — one structure + one signature, one dominant (film-craft ch. 9 logic).
